Local ground truth

Why sewer lines in central Oklahoma have a hard life

Drain problems here have local causes. The soil, the weather and the age of the house each do their own kind of damage.

topsoil
expansive red clay
Permian red bed shale
01

Shrink-swell clay

Much of the metro sits on clay that swells when it's wet and shrinks and cracks in a dry August. That constant movement works pipe joints loose and can sag a line into a belly where waste sits.

02

Thirsty trees

Elms, silver maples, sweetgums and Bradford pears go after the moisture in your sewer line during a drought. A hairline gap in a joint is all it takes for roots to get in and fill the pipe.

03

Aging materials

Older neighborhoods like Mesta Park, Crown Heights, Heritage Hills and the Paseo still have clay tile, cast iron and sometimes Orangeburg pipe that is long past its expected life.

Questions

Things people ask us before the camera goes in

Doesn't my home inspector already check the sewer line?

Usually not. A standard home inspection runs the faucets and flushes the toilets, but nobody looks inside the buried line. A sewer repair can run from a few thousand dollars to well over $10,000, so a scope during your option period is cheap insurance.

How long does an inspection take?

Most homes take 45 to 75 minutes. You're welcome to watch the monitor with us. We'll explain what we're seeing as we go.

What if there's no cleanout?

We can often get in through a roof vent or by pulling a toilet. If there's no reasonable access, we'll tell you before we start, and you won't pay for a trip that goes nowhere.

Who's responsible for the line, me or the city?

In Oklahoma City, the homeowner is generally responsible for the service lateral all the way to where it ties into the public main, including the part under the street or easement. Our report marks where the tie-in is.

Will you fix what you find?

No, and that's on purpose. We only inspect, so we have no reason to exaggerate. Take our report to any licensed plumber for a repair bid. We're happy to come back and verify the repair afterward.

How often should I have my line scoped?

Every two to three years for older homes with large trees nearby, right before you buy any house, and any time you have a backup. Newer PVC lines can go much longer.
